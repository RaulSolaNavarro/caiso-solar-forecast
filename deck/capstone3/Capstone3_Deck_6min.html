What it means for the grid

A smaller error means less backup power to hold

To be covered in 95 of 100 daylight hours: about 3,630 MW of backup with our forecast, 4,790 MW with the simple guess. The error gap is worth about 3 TWh and $120 million a year: a size, not a savings promise.

Today's fleet: EIA 860M nameplate Sep 2026, all CISO, 26,034.5 MW. Hours when solar was turned down count as shortfalls here, which overstates CAISO's bar most. Price: $41 per MWh, CAISO 2024 average day-ahead.

Limits and next steps

What we learned, where it stops, what to do next

What we learnedFree data gets close to the pros

A simple model on public data comes close to CAISO's own forecast and holds up best when it is cloudy.

LimitsSpring and summer only

The test has no winter. Spring 2026 had far more turned-down solar than spring 2025, so we forecast about 500 MW too high on average. History is 2.6 years.

What we recommendRefit often, use as a second opinion

Refit every month, watch turned-down solar, use it next to CAISO's forecast, and add winter data as it arrives.

Winter was checked only in practice runs (Dec to Feb error 3.8% to 4.9%). Sources: EIA, CAISO, Open-Meteo.com (CC BY 4.0).
