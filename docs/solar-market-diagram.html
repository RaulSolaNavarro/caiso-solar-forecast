  • 2Day before: offers go in
    • ✓
      Window: opens 7 days ahead, closes 10 a.m. Pacific the day beforeCAISO market rules, section 30.5.1.
    • ✓
      Scheduling coordinators send offers for plants and for buyersIn the PG&E contract it is the seller or its coordinator. The contract decides.
    • ✓
      A plant offers with a price, or as a self-schedule with no priceSelf-schedules sit at the start of the supply curve.
    • ✓

    • ✓
      CAISO makes its own demand, solar and wind forecastsDay-ahead net load = demand forecast minus solar and wind forecast.
  • 3CAISO matches everything at once
    • ✓
      Picks the cheapest mix for the whole gridNot buyer to plant. Contracts are not an input to the match.
    • ✓
      One price per place and hourPlants are paid at their own spot. Buyers pay a zone price: PG&E, SCE, SDG&E or VEA.
    • ✓
      Bids are checked for market power2024: about 22% (1,060 MW) of day-ahead bids subject to mitigation were changed.
    • ✓
      Adds capacity if its demand forecast is higher than what was scheduledResidual unit commitment.
    • ?
      Results come out about 1 p.m.CAISO's timeline marks 13:00, but we did not find the publication time stated.
  • 4Who pays whom
    • ✓
      Plant is paid the day-ahead price at its spotCAISO market rules, section 11.2.1.
    • ✓
      Buyer pays the day-ahead price for its loadCharged at its load zone.
    • ✓
      The contract settles the gap, so the buyer pays the contract priceCAISO calls it an Inter-SC trade: an optional settlement service for bilateral energy.
    • =
      Example at a $40 contractMarket $25: buyer tops up $15. Market $60: plant returns $20. Buyer pays $40 either way.
    • ?
      No electrons are routed from plant to buyerThe contract moves the money. Follows from how settlement works; no source states it in these words.
  • 5The day itself: real-time market
    • ✓
      Every 15 and 5 minutes CAISO sends each plant a new targetReal-time market, "plant reset".
    • ✓
      Gaps from the day-ahead schedule settle at the real-time priceCAISO market rules, sections 11.1(e) and 11.5.2.
    • ≈
      Solar short: buy the gap. Solar extra: sell it, sometimes below $0Who bears the cost depends on the contract and the scheduling coordinator.
    • ✓
      Cost signal: real-time imbalance offsets were $205 million in 2025Solar misses are one cause among several (CAISO monitor, 2025 report).
    • ≈
      This is where a solar miss costs moneyA better day-ahead solar forecast shrinks the gap.
  • 6Too much power at midday
    • ✓
      Ladder: batteries charge, power goes to neighbors, other plants back down, then solar is turned downBatteries: 8.0 GW (2023) to 11.6 GW (2024). Neighbors avoided 274 GWh of turn-downs in 2024 (EIA).
    • ✓
      2025 turn-downs: 89% from farms' own bids, 9% from self-scheduled farms4,780 GWh and 480 GWh (CAISO monitor, 2025 report, p. 2).
    • ✓
      Not a light switchA plant controller sends each inverter a lower target. Some are turned down, others shut off.
    • ✓
      2024: 3.4 million MWh of wind and solar turned down, 93% solarEIA, May 28, 2025.
  • 7Rooftop solar (behind the meter)
    • ✓
      The home uses it first; extra flows to the gridExcess is energy above the host customer's own load (CAISO, 2018 proposal).
    • ✓

    • ≈
      More than expected means lower demand than plannedThe surplus then goes up the ladder in branch 6.
    • ✓
